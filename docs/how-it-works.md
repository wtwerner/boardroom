# How Boardroom works

The product in detail, for anyone changing it: how the group uses each part, where the data comes from, and where the code lives. The site's own **How it works** page (`src/app/(app)/about/page.tsx`) explains the numbers to members; keep both in step when a score or vote rule changes.

## How it's used

Every member can do everything with the group's data, including logging, deleting and merging: it's meant for a small group that trusts each other. Admins also look after Group settings and who's a member. With casual sign-in on, the casual night's players can have accounts that only see their own night (see [Who can sign in](#who-can-sign-in) and [Casual accounts](#casual-accounts)).

**First visit (each member).**
1. Sign in with Discord. You're matched to your name in past plays: by Discord id if you were @-mentioned, otherwise by name, when only one player has it (with two, you start fresh and merge yours in). If the group already renamed that player ("Sam" for a handle like "sambo99"), you keep their name, not your Discord one. The first sign-in lands on **Get set up** (`/welcome`).
2. Get set up walks through each step on one page, with the forms right there:
   - **Check your name.** It starts as your Discord name; most people switch to their first name. Saving it, changed or not, ticks the step (`user.nameChosenAt`).
   - **Find yourself in past plays.** This appears only when nothing is linked to you yet. Old posts may call you "Alex" while you signed in as "alexb". Merge the old name into you.
   - **Rate the games you've played** (Rankings, then Rate games): one tap per game, from 😍 to 🙅, plus *Want to play* and 🔥 *Must play*.
   - **Rank them**, a few "this or that?" questions each, then drag to adjust. Heavy and light games are separate lists, so the step has a button for each and is done when each list you've played games in is fully ranked or has 10 in it (`rankLists` in `src/lib/setup.ts`). Rankings points to the other list once there's nothing new in the one you're on.
   - **Add your BoardGameGeek collection** and **your Board Game Arena name**. Both optional, and both spelled out, since the two are easy to mix up: BGG is where people list the games they own, BGA is for playing online. BGG takes your username, or the CSV export until the BGG API token is approved.

   It also says where events are posted and lets you pick your meeple color. The name, rating and ranking are the steps that count (`src/lib/setup.ts`): until they're done, Home shows one line with how far along you are and the next step, and taps through to Get set up. It never puts the whole checklist on Home. Settings links to the page too.

   Later, when tastes have moved on, **Replay this or that** (on Rankings) ranks everything again from scratch: your old list stays hidden, every answer feeds a Bradley–Terry fit (`src/lib/pairwise.ts`) so one odd pick doesn't sink a game, and nothing changes until you save the result.
3. Settings also has your display name and meeple color. Colors can repeat (a new account gets one of the least used, `pickColor` in `src/lib/meeple.ts`), so a meeple only appears beside a name. Where nothing else says who someone is (a game's owners on Shelf, each person's chance on Discover and Online, votes on an event's games, Group taste), the site shows their Discord picture in a ring of their color, or their initial without one (`Face` and `Faces` in `src/components/ui.tsx`). A row with one for each person shows the five a game suits best, then how many more, once there are more than six.

**New games to rank.** Once you're set up, a game you play that isn't in your ranking yet shows three ways: a count beside **Rankings** in the nav (and on the tab bar on phones), a *New to rank* card at the top of Home naming the games and when you last played one, with a button to the right list, and a count on each list's switch on Rankings. Ranking them clears all three.

**Two rankings.** Games are ranked against their own kind: heavy games (BGG weight 2.75 and up, the longer, heavier games) and light games. The split is by the game, not the night it was played: Barcelona at casual night is still a heavy game. A game's page can move it to the other list (`game.tier`); otherwise `src/lib/tier.ts` decides from its weight. The weight is a [setting](../README.md#your-groups-settings). The lists are named for weight rather than for a night, since any night can play either. Every use of rankings (group lists, the planner, Discover's taste model, Claude's context) counts a game's place within its own list. With a casual night, each kind of night also has its own group list (see [Core and casual nights](#core-and-casual-nights)), and a game's page says where it stands in each.

**Between game nights.**
- **Plan** lists every upcoming night by date, each with where its planning stands. Add a night on Plan, or with `/event new` in Discord, which opens a form: the kind of night (starting as the kind whose channel you're in), date, time, title and place, with the last night's time and place filled in. Each night has its own page, so two nights planned at once don't get in each other's way.
- **Each night is posted to Discord** when the group has set that up ([Nights in Discord](discord-setup.md#part-4-nights-in-discord-optional)): a card in the night's events channel (pinging whoever the group set for that kind of night, once) with when and where (shown in each reader's own time zone), who's coming, and the plan so far, plus a thread for talking it over. Its buttons answer Going, Maybe or Can't make it (the symbols the card heads each list with), **Someone else…** answers for someone in the server or someone named who isn't in Discord, and **Edit** (or `/event edit`) changes the title, date, time, place or seats. Answering or changing the night on the site updates the post, and the post's buttons update the site.
  - **Seats.** A night can have a seat limit, set when it's made (Seats on the site's form, or `/event new seats:6`, since Discord's form has no room for a sixth field) or with Edit. Whoever says Going once the seats are taken (counting the guests people bring and the others counted by hand) waits for a seat: the page and the post list them under *Waiting for a seat*, and the post's count says "6 of 6 seats, full". Nobody's answer changes, so a seat that opens goes to whoever's waited longest (`fillSeats` in `src/lib/rsvp.ts`), and the thread tells them they're going. The reminder before the night leaves out anyone still waiting.
  - In a forum, each night is a post of its own, whose buttons work as they are. With an announcements channel set for its kind of night on Group settings, it's also announced in that chat channel, with when and where, how many are coming, the game, and a link to the post. The announcement is edited along with the post and takes the night's ping instead of the post. While a night's finding its date the announcement lists the dates on offer, and when one's set the reply goes on it (`announce_message_id`).
  - In a text channel, Discord shows the post at the top of its thread without working buttons, so the thread's first message is a short copy whose buttons work: a line of counts (or each date on offer and how many can come, while the date's TBD) and the post's buttons, without the card, so it doesn't read as the post twice. It's kept in step with the post (`thread_card_id`; a forum post needs none), and a post from before there were copies gets one at the bottom of its thread the next time it changes.
  - A new night is named from the group's template (New events' title on Group settings: "10/29 Game Night — TBD"), which the site's form fills in from the date; a name made that way swaps in the game when one's locked in, and back to TBD when it's unlocked. The thread has the night's name, with its date put in front when the name doesn't start with it ("10/29 Thursday heavy"). When a night moves, the thread is told what changed and renamed.
  - Answering Going or Maybe (in either place) adds you to the thread, so it shows under the channel in your channel list; Discord archives a thread after a quiet week, and anyone posting in it opens it again.
  - `/event cancel` marks the post cancelled; deleting a night on the site takes its post down. A few hours before it starts (Reminder on Group settings, 3 by default), the post gets a reply pinging everyone going or a maybe, and reminding anyone bringing games to bring them (the post says which); a night that moves is reminded again for its new time.
  - **Add to calendar**, on a night's page and its post, once it has a date. The web can't see someone's default calendar app, so a page (`/calendar/<key>`) offers a calendar file, which on an iPhone opens the Calendar app's add screen and on Android whichever app takes the file, and links to Google's and Outlook's add screens, with the likeliest first for their phone. That adds the night as it is then, and doesn't follow changes. The page needs no sign-in, since most of a casual night's people have no account: its address has the night's id signed with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, so nobody can step through the others. An event runs for **How long an event runs** on Group settings (3 hours by default), since nights only have a start.
  - **Sync my events** subscribes someone's calendar to a feed of every night they're going to, might go to, or are waiting for a seat at. It follows moves, renames and cancellations (a cancelled night stays, marked cancelled), and a night leaves when they say they can't make it or it's deleted. Calendar apps can't sign in, so the feed's address holds a secret per player (`player.calendar_token`, made when they first ask, by player so people without an account get one too). A post's Add to calendar button answers privately with that person's links, since a link button on the post couldn't say who pressed it; it doesn't add them as a player, so someone who hasn't answered anything gets the one-off link and is told how to sync. That link also goes in the address of the event's calendar page they open from Discord, whose browser isn't signed in to the site. Members also have **Sync my events** on Plan, the event's calendar page and Settings. **New sync link** on that answer, or **Get a new link** on Settings, replaces the secret and the old address stops working. Calendars check on their own schedule, so changes can take hours to show, up to about a day on Google, and Google's phone app can't subscribe by itself, so the page explains doing it in a browser.
- **Finding a date.** When the group doesn't know when the next night is, anyone offers a few dates: **Find a date** on Plan, where you tap them on a calendar (days with an event already are dotted, and they all start at the kind of night's usual time, the last one's, unless one's changed), with a day to decide by if you like, or `/event poll` in Discord, which opens a form for up to 8 dates (`thu, sat 2pm, 11/7`: a date without its own time starts at the form's time), a title and a place (and, with a casual night, which kind of night it's for: the one whose channel `/event poll` was run in, to start with). That makes the night straight away, with its date TBD (`game_night.finding_date`): it has its own page, its post (in that kind of night's events channel, without the night ping, which waits for the date; an announcement of it links to the night's page to help plan it) and its thread from the start, and keeps all three once the date's set, so nothing moves. Plan lists it under *Picking a date*; games can be proposed or brought before there's a date. The page and the post show each date with who can come.
  - **Answers.** Each date takes *Works*, *If I have to* or *Can't* on the site; the post's **Works for me** beside each date turns works on, or off to can't, and **My answers** opens a panel only you see with a menu of all three on each date, and **None of these work**. Once someone has answered any date, the dates they left alone count as can't for them, except dates added after their last answer, which they haven't seen yet. **None work** says can't to every date on offer. The rules are in `src/lib/date-answers.ts`.
  - **Who's answered.** It waits on the members (signed in, either kind of night: a casual night's guests can answer, but it doesn't wait on them). The page and the post say "4 of 6 answered · waiting on Alex and Casey", counting someone as waiting until they've answered every date still to come, and each date says *everyone can come*, *best so far* (the top date with enough people; when two or more are level on who can come and on maybes, none is called best) or *too few* (fewer than **Fewest to set a date** on Group settings, 2 for the group's own nights and 3 for a casual night by default; anyone who can come counts toward it). Once the last member answers, the thread says which date's best, that the top dates tie, or that none has enough and pings whoever started it to set it; a date added later starts that over. **Nudge them in Discord** on the site pings whoever's still waiting in the thread, at most every 12 hours (`date_poll.everyone_answered_at`, `nudged_at`). Home lists the events waiting on you under *Which dates work for you?*.
  - **Set the date** (on the site or the post) lists the dates best first: the most who can come (works or if they have to), then the fewest who'd only come if they have to, then the soonest. Setting one dates the night, and everyone who answered is down as they did: works is going, if I have to is a maybe, can't is can't make it, in the order they answered. The post becomes the night's card, its thread is renamed with the date, and a reply pings the group's setting with the date and a link to RSVP, plus, underneath, those going or a maybe. Title and place were set when it was made (Edit changes them while the date's TBD), so setting it asks only which date.
  - **Date fell through** (on the night's page or **Find a new date** on its post), with a few more dates to offer if you like, puts the night back to finding one. Nothing's lost: that date stays on the list, crossed out as fallen through, with the night's answers as its answers (going is works, and so on), the other dates keep everyone's answers to them, and the games, votes and prep stay. Answers to a date that fell through don't count toward the "left alone counts as can't" rule. A night made with a date gets its list of dates now. The post shows the dates again, and a note in the thread says which date fell through and pings whoever was going.
  - **Decide by.** Whoever starts it can give a day to decide by (on Find a date, or Edit while it's TBD). At noon that day the tick pings them in the thread with the best date so far and who it's still waiting on, once (`date_poll.decide_by`, `decide_pinged_at`; a new day pings again). It doesn't set the date for them.
  - Anyone can **Suggest a date** (it's offered to everyone who's answered as not answered yet), **Call it off**, or **Reopen** one called off while one of its dates is still coming up. When every date on offer has gone by, the tick says so on the post, which keeps Suggest a date and Call it off. Answers follow who the night is for, like a night's: a core night's are members only (`DISCORD_MEMBER_ROLE_ID`), a casual night's anyone in the server's. They're stored by player (`date_poll`, `date_poll_option`, `date_poll_vote`, one poll per night). A night still finding its date is left out of Home, the reminders, the morning-after DM and `/event edit` and `cancel`, and has no date for plays to match.
- **Who's coming** is an answer per person: Going, Maybe or Can't make it, plus anyone they're bringing who isn't named. Answer on the night's page or with the post's buttons. Anyone can answer for someone else, on the site or with the post's **Someone else…** (a member who said so in chat, say), name someone new who isn't on Boardroom or in Discord yet, and count others coming by hand. Someone named this way is a player from then on: their plays match them by name, and when they sign in or first use the post's buttons under that name, the player becomes theirs. Whoever makes a night answers like everyone else (making it doesn't mean going), and is added to its thread. The core group's nights can be answered by members (`DISCORD_MEMBER_ROLE_ID`, when it's set); the casual night by anyone in the server, so casual players answer for themselves without an account.
- On a night's page, the recommendations start from who's going (anyone going who isn't a member counts as an other). The picker scores the shelf using the ratings, rankings and replay votes of the people coming, how recently each game was played, and its best player count. Claude can suggest picks too.
- **The group's nights** decide the game together: anyone **proposes** games from the recommendations, everyone taps *I'd play it* on any they would, and anyone **locks one in**. The locked-in game gets its prep: BGG's rules and how-to-play videos are linked automatically, anyone can add a teach video or player aid, and everyone taps **Mark my prep done** so you know who's ready. The links people add are the game's (`game.prep_links`), not the night's: they're under *Rules and teach* on the game's page, where they can be added too, and they're there again the next time it's locked in. BGG's links need the game's name in the path (`src/lib/bgg-links.ts`), or BGG shows the game's overview instead of its files or videos.
- **Casual nights**, if the group has them, have a bring list instead: tap *I'll bring it* on a game you own and it shows under your name, so nobody brings the same box twice. Only someone who owns a game can bring it, so for one you don't, the line under it reads *Ask Sam or Alex to bring theirs* (people coming first): tap a name to ask them. A game you own only offers *I'll bring it*. It goes on the list as asked, the bot DMs them with **I'll bring it** and **Can't this time**, and they can answer there or on the night's page. Yes moves it under their name; no takes it off the list. Saying you'll bring your own copy answers an open ask. Anyone can take a game off the list. The recommendations show whose copies are coming and lean toward games someone coming can teach.
- The dashboard shows the nights coming up and where each stands.
- **Past nights** are listed below, newest first. A past night's page shows who came (from the plays, beside who said they were going), what was played, and how that compares with the plan: whether the locked-in game was played, or which of the games brought were. Home's last game night and each night on Plays link to it. Nights made from plays are named for their date with the year ("9/28/25 Game Night"), and a night from another year shows its year where the date badge has the weekday.
- **Want to play** is "I'd like this on the table soon", played it or not. It counts toward a game's "N want to play" and the picker, and it clears once you play the game, so the counts are about what people want next. Tick it again if you still want to.
- **🔥 Must play** is for "I really want this on the table". Each person holds 3 at a time. They count separately from want to play: one person's strong wish is different from several people being mildly keen. They weigh heavily in the picker, and a vote comes back once you play that game.

  Only a play dated on or after a vote clears it, so importing an old night from Discord leaves today's votes alone.
- **Discover.** Every game nobody owns is scored against each person's taste profile (`src/lib/taste.ts`): the mechanics, categories and weights they rank and rate highly. `src/lib/chance.ts` turns that into a percentage: the chance they'd rank the game above their usual game, checked against the games they've already rated (it counts once someone has rated or ranked 6). The group's number is the average of everyone's chances. *Claude's picks* (with `CLAUDE=on`) sends that analysis to Claude for tailored ideas, for the group or just for you. Each one is checked on BGG and scored the same way, and the most confident (up to 8 for the group, 6 for you, each at 60% or better) replace the list, so asking again follows the latest ratings and rankings. Claude can keep a pick that still fits; one someone tapped "Want it" on stays on the buy list (`src/lib/picks.ts`). *Group taste* (on People) shows the statistics behind it. "Want it" puts a game on the buy list, and tapping it again takes your vote back (it comes off once nobody wants it); *Take it off* on the buy list removes it for everyone. "Not for me" stops it being suggested to you.
- **Coming up** (a tab of its own on Discover, left out while there's nothing on it) is games nobody owns, all from BGG's API, with no button or Claude, only the BGG token (`src/lib/upcoming.ts`). It has three groups:
  - *Opening soon*: a [geeklist BGG members keep](https://boardgamegeek.com/geeklist/290810/prelaunched-crowdfunding-board-game-projects) of campaigns yet to open. Most entries say "TBA", so most have no date. An entry drops off after its date's last month, or with no date, a year after anyone last touched it. The text is written by hand, so `parsePrelaunchBody` reads what it can and leaves out a date it can't.
  - *Crowdfunded*: the newest 650 games in each of BGG's Crowdfunding: Kickstarter and Gamefound families (ids go up as games are added), dated this year or later or with no year yet. That reaches back far enough for games still funding, which are often added to BGG months before.
  - *Trending and wishlisted*: games on BGG's hot list or someone's wishlist dated this year or later, or on the hot list with no year yet.

  Up to 6 show in each group per night, each at 50% or better, scored as on Discover except without BGG's rating, which comes mostly from backers and fans this early. A game with no weight on BGG yet (most of them) shows under every night it seats; one with no mechanics or categories either has no percentage and goes last. Games only from these sources stay out of Discover's other tabs; a trending or wishlisted game shows on them too, as it always did. BGG can't say whether a campaign is funding right now or when it ends, or whether a game dated this year is out yet, so each card links to the game's BGG page. Crowdfunding sites have no feed and refuse scripts.

  `refreshUpcoming` (`src/lib/catalog.ts`) rewrites `upcoming_game` nightly, fetching details for 200 games a run, so a new site's list fills out over the first week or so. Expansions in the families are remembered in `sync_state` so they aren't asked about again.
- **Shelf of shame.** Games someone owns that the group has never played, best bets first by the group's chance of liking them (as Discover scores them). Legacy games, games for 2 at most and games enough of the group turned down are left out. Home shows the top ten; Shelf's *Shelf of shame* toggle shows them all.

**Online.** The Online page lists the group's games that are on Board Game Arena: played together and worth another go, on the shelf but never played, and wishlist or buy-list games to try before buying. *Recommended on BGA* scores BGA games nobody owns or has played with the group against everyone's taste, as Discover does (BGG details for BGA's 400 most played games are fetched 100 a night). Tap *I'm up for it* on the ones you'd play there (game pages have the same toggle). Add your BGA name in Settings so the others can find you (casual accounts can add theirs too, listed separately), and tick *I have BGA Premium* if you do: only whoever opens the table needs it for a Premium game. Plays on BGA aren't logged here; BGA has no API for them.

**How it works** (in the sidebar, or the header's ⋯ menu on phones) explains every number on the site in plain words: rankings, Discover's percentages, votes, the planner's points and what Claude sees.

**On the night.** Log what's played on the night's page as it's played. Once the night starts it's *Happening now*, with a live dot, on its page, its card on Home and Plan (where it goes first, above the nights picking a date) and Home's top story, which shows what's been played so far. Its page is then only *What's been played*, with *Rules and teach* under it (BGG's rules and how-to-play videos, plus any prep links, for the game locked in, or on a casual night the games brought that haven't been played): answering, the plan, the bring list and the recommendations are behind it. It has the games planned or brought to tap (the one locked in first, each saying where it stood) (so one brought and never opened isn't logged), *Something else…* for a game that wasn't on the list, and who's playing (who said they were going, then whoever played the last game). Tapping a game logs it at once, at the end of the night's list, and everyone with the page open sees it within a few seconds (the page refreshes when the night's plays change). The arrows beside each game fix the order, and its pencil who played it. *That's everything* ends the night there and then (`game_night.ended_at`); otherwise it's over 12 hours after it starts. Either way the page then shows who came, what was played and how that went against the plan, with logging another game tucked under it. The morning after (10am to 9pm in the group's time zone, up to three days on), whoever made the night gets a DM linking there if nothing's been logged for it yet, hand-written posts included. After a casual night, once something's logged for it, everyone with an account who played gets a DM too, naming the games they played and haven't rated, with a link to Rate games (`sendRateNudges`, once a night: `game_night.rate_nudged_at`), so the next one's planner has more to go on. On Plays, add each game you played (*Another game that night*), with the date, place and players once for all of them. Anyone can log. Use *Someone new* for a guest; a name someone on the list already has asks whether it's them or someone new. Each game is saved as its own play, and *Post it to Discord* puts the night in the channel as one message, with everyone tagged: the night's log post (`src/lib/log-post.ts`). Games logged later for the same night, on its page or on Plays, are added to that message rather than posted again, in the night's order (`play.position`, set when someone moves a game; otherwise the order they were logged), with a link button per game, five to a row. When the night has a thread, the log post links to it, and the thread gets a copy of the log post that's kept in step with it (`game_night.log_copy_id`). A play's night is its kind of night on its date; a date with no night yet gets one, so every night has a page. Logging a game marks its plan as played, and clears the 🔥 and want-to-play votes of everyone who played it.

**Hand-written Discord posts still work.** The nightly sync, or the **Sync from Discord** button, imports them. If a play was logged in both places, the sync skips the Discord copy. The log form also checks Discord first and asks before saving a second play of the same game on the same day. Editing or deleting a play the app posted rewrites its Discord message, or deletes it with the night's last game. When someone edits a hand-written post (to add a game they forgot, say), the next sync picks that up for two weeks afterward.

## Core and casual nights

The casual night is optional: an admin turns it on with **A casual night too** on Group settings. Without it the site has one kind of night, and none of the switches, color splits or casual-night copy below appear. Code that's only about the casual night checks `CASUAL_NIGHT` from `src/config.ts`.

With it, every play says which night it was: the core group's, or the casual night, where the group plays lighter games with more casual players. Each night has its own Discord channel, and the sync reads both. Plays and Discover's group picks have a switch between the two nights; Plan lists both kinds of night together.

- **Both nights count** toward what each member has played (so it's theirs to rate and rank), clear votes when someone plays a game, and go into the shelf's play counts and what Claude sees (with casual plays marked).
- **Home counts both nights**, split by color in its numbers and charts. Its last event is the latest night of either kind.
- **Planning the casual night** counts only its own plays for recency and "never played", starts with light games, gives a nudge to games it keeps coming back to, and drops games nobody coming has played, since guests learn at the table. Plans belong to a night, so a casual play of a game doesn't tick off the core group's plan for it.
- **Group rankings by night.** Each kind of night has its own group list on Rankings, from the rankings of the people who come to it, so the two rooms can be compared game for game: the core group's is every member's, and the casual night's is everyone who's played at one in the last year (`CROWD_DAYS` in `src/lib/ranking.ts`), members included. Same games, same scoring; only whose lists count changes (`getNightRankingLists` in `src/lib/queries.ts`). Home's top games stay the core group's.
- **One shelf, everyone's games.** Shelf has every account's collection, members' and casual accounts' alike, so anyone can see what the whole group owns (`getWholeShelf` in `src/lib/queries.ts`). Its owner filter narrows to one person, to the casual night's regulars (everyone who's played at one in the last year, and every casual account: what could come to the table there) or, for members, to the core group. The shelf of shame only has the core group's games, since it's scored against the members' taste. Discover, the buy list and Home's shelf stay the core group's (`getShelf`). BGG lists some editions as separate games (Citadels from 2000 and 2016), so the shelf counts editions as one game, with everyone's copies, every edition's plays and everyone's opinions, under the edition played most (`mergeEditions` in `src/lib/editions.ts`). Editions are games BGG links as reimplementations that also have the same name: the link alone would join new games built on an old one (Citadels Duel), and the name alone two unrelated games. A play logged against one edition, even one nobody owns, doesn't leave someone's copy looking unplayed. The links come with a game's BGG details (`game.implementations`); games detailed before they were kept are fetched again by the nightly sync, 60 a night.
- **Discover by night.** Weight is what sets the nights apart, so each Discover tab only shows its kind of game: heavy games (weight 2.75 and up) for the core group, light games for the casual night, which also leaves out games heavier than 4 in 5 of the ones it has played (weight 2.5 until it has played 8 games with a BGG weight). Each tab uses its night's usual table size and has its own list of Claude's picks, asked for with that night in mind. Until the casual night has plays, its table is everyone plus two guests. Both nights are worked out together, so switching tabs is instant.
- **Casual players get accounts when the group wants them.** Without casual sign-in they're names in the logs, linked to their Discord account when they're @-mentioned, and posts the app makes in the casual channel don't link to the site, since most of that channel couldn't open it. With **Casual night players can sign in** on Group settings (named for the night), anyone in the server can sign in with an account for the casual night only, and the casual night's posts and log posts link to the site (see [Casual accounts](#casual-accounts)). `pnpm refresh:posts` and `pnpm refresh:logs` add the link to posts already up.
- **Moving up to the core group's nights.** Everyone's at the casual night already, so this is an extra, not joining a group. When a casual regular wants in on the heavier nights, an admin invites them: **People → Invite someone to …**, right-click them in Discord → **Apps → Invite to …** (both named for the core group's night), or **Member** on People and access for someone who already has a casual account. Each asks once (or, on People and access, does it on the tap), then gives them the member role, says hi in the core group's channel (the welcome channel on Group settings, or the core events channel; never a casual night channel) with a *Get set up* button, and DMs them the setup steps with a link to Get set up. People lists them as invited until they sign in, and their casual night history comes with them. Giving the role needs the bot to have Manage Roles ([Part 5](discord-setup.md#part-5-inviting-people-to-the-core-groups-nights-optional)); a group without a member role gets the hello and the DM. The right-click command only shows for people who can manage roles until the owner opens it to the member role.

## Group settings and admins

What a group sets for itself (its name, time zone, kinds of night, the Discord channels and roles) is on **Settings → Group settings**, stored in the `setting` table (`src/lib/settings.ts` checks each value; `src/lib/settings-store.ts` reads and writes them). Only admins (`user.is_admin`) can open it, and they can make other members admins. Admins also have **Settings → People and access** (`/settings/access`, not found for anyone else): everyone with an account by kind (members, casual accounts, off the site), with a switch for each and an admin box, what each kind can do (`capabilities` in `src/lib/access.ts`, which a test holds to the actions), where Discord's member role disagrees, and the casual night's regulars who have no account and whether they're in the Discord server. While nobody's an admin, on a new site or after the last one leaves, every member can, so someone can make themselves the first. The last admin can't step down, so the settings don't fall open by accident.

`src/config.ts` keeps the settings everything else reads. `loadSettings()` fills it from the table: pages and layouts read a cached copy, which saving Group settings expires (`updateTag("settings")`), so pages built ahead of time are built again with the new values; `requireUser()`, Discord's interactions, the crons and scripts read the table itself. A setting never saved falls back to the environment variable sites used before the page existed (`SITE_NAME`, `APP_TIMEZONE`, `DISCORD_PLAYS_CHANNEL_ID` and so on), so an older site keeps working until an admin first saves.

## Who can sign in

Signing in is with Discord, and only for people in the group's server (`DISCORD_GUILD_ID`), checked on every sign-in, so leaving the server ends it. A Discord account without an email (one made with a phone number) signs in like any other; Boardroom never emails anyone.

**Boardroom decides what someone is, not Discord** (`signInAccess` in `src/lib/access.ts`, applied in `src/lib/auth.ts`). The first time someone signs in, the member role on Group settings decides: with it they're a member; without it they get a casual account when **Casual night players can sign in** is on, and a plain "couldn't sign you in" when it isn't. After that, it's what their account says (`user.access`): admins move people up, down or off the site on People and access, and Discord's member role follows (given or taken by the bot, which needs Manage Roles). Someone taken off the site is signed out at once, gets the same plain "couldn't sign you in", and counts for nothing. A role changed by hand in Discord doesn't change anything here: People and access shows it under **Discord doesn't match**, with **Match Discord** to put the role back in step. The bot's buttons and `/event` go by the same thing (`accessOf` in `src/lib/discord-handlers.ts`): an account's access, or for someone who hasn't signed in, what their first sign-in would make them. So a casual account holding the role still can't answer for, make or change the core group's events, and someone taken off the site can't answer the casual night's either.

Without a member role, anyone in the server signs in as a member, which suits a server that's just the group, and Settings warns about it, since every member can delete and merge. With casual sign-in on, new people start casual instead and an admin moves them up. In a bigger server, or one the casual night's players share, choose the role.

### Casual accounts

Each account is a member, a casual account or off the site (`user.access`, `ACCESS` in `src/db/enums.ts`). A casual account comes from signing in without the member role while casual sign-in is on, or from an admin moving a member down.

**The rule.** Each kind of night has its own events, polls, plays, group list and Discord channels. Access decides which kinds someone sees (`nightsFor` and `seesNight` in `src/lib/session.ts`): a member sees both, a casual account the casual night. Everything else on the site is everyone's, shown through the nights they see. On top of that, two things go by role, not by night: making and running events, and the group's housekeeping, are the members'; Group settings and access are the admins'. People and access has the whole table (`capabilities` in `src/lib/access.ts`, which `access.test.ts` holds to each action's check).

- **Hidden from a casual account:** the core group's events, polls and plays (their pages and actions answer as if they were gone), the core group's list on Rankings, and anything that names the core group as a group. What's sent to the browser follows: a casual account's pages carry the casual night's name, not the core group's.
- **Theirs, on the casual night:** answering for themselves or someone they're bringing, counting others coming, saying which dates work and suggesting one, *I'll bring it* and asking someone to bring a game, voting and prep, Claude's picks for the event, logging what's played and ending the night. Actions on a night check `requireNight` (or the night themselves); the casual night's Claude context is its own (`buildGroupContext` with `night`), so it never describes the core group.
- **Members' only:** making, editing, dating, calling off and deleting events and locking in the game (`requireMember()`); housekeeping, which changes things for everyone: other people's plays, syncing from Discord, fixing game names, merging or splitting players, moving a game between the ranking lists, taking prep links off and loading BGG's and BGA's lists; and Discover's buy list, adding ideas and Claude's picks there, which are written from the core group's taste.
- **What they see, page by page.** The same nav as everyone.
  - **Home** is their night's: what's on now and coming up, its events still picking a date with whether they've answered, the last one's games, its favorites, and their setup or new games to rank.
  - **Plan** lists the casual night's events and polls, without the forms to make one. **Plays** has the casual night's plays and its play form, with edit and delete on the ones they logged, and none of the housekeeping queues.
  - **Shelf** is everyone's games, ratings and votes included, narrowed to the casual night's regulars or one person. **Rankings** has only the casual night's list.
  - **People** is the casual night's people (`getNightPeople`: everyone who's played at one in the last year, and every casual account), counted at its nights; a person's page shows what they've played there. **Group taste** is theirs too.
  - **Discover** is the casual night's view and *For you*, scored for the casual night's people with everyone's games as the shelf (`getRecommendations` with `crowd`). **Online** is everyone with an account, the casual night's plays, and no buy list.
  - **Game pages** have what everyone who's said something thinks (a member also sees every member), where the game stands in the casual night's list, and its casual night plays. Anyone can add a prep link.
  - **How it works** is their part of it, **Settings** and **Get set up** have their own things.
- **Seeing it as they would.** With a casual night, Settings has **See it as a casual night player** (named for the night) for members: a cookie (`casual-preview`, a week) that makes `requireUser()` treat them as a casual account everywhere, pages and actions alike, so what the group checks is what casual players get. A bar at the top of every page says so, with **Back to the full site**. Their own data still counts as a member's.
- **What counts as the group.** `inCrowd` in `src/lib/queries.ts` keeps the core group's own numbers to members (Home's shelf, its group list, its Discover, People, Online and taste, the buy list and the shelf of shame), so a casual player's collection or ratings never change the core group's; the casual night's versions are its own people's. An event's planner goes by who's coming, whatever their account: its people are the night's crowd (members for the core group's, everyone for the casual night) plus anyone with an account who's answered going, and it uses their taste, rankings and games, as does Claude's context. The casual night's group list counts casual players once they've played at one, and the Shelf page shows everyone's games. A poll waits on its night's people: the members for the core group's, the casual night's people for its own (`waitsOn` in `src/lib/poll-store.ts`). Only a member can be an admin.
- **Moving up.** Inviting someone with a casual account to the core group's nights makes them a member there and then, with their ratings, rankings and collection as they were. If the bot can't give the role, they're a member anyway, and People and access shows the mismatch.
- **Moving down, or off the site.** Moving a member down takes them off the core group's upcoming events (`leaveNights` in `src/lib/night-store.ts`): their answers, the dates they said work, their votes and prep, with each event's post updated and anyone waiting for a seat told they have one. People and access asks first. Taking someone off the site does the same for every upcoming event, and also drops the games they said they'd bring and the asks waiting on them. Events that have started keep everything (history, or tonight's, where they are anyway), and their ratings, rankings and collection stay, so letting them back in (as either) picks up where they were.
- **Without a casual night.** Turning off *A casual night too* keeps casual accounts and everything of theirs, but they can't sign in, and one already signed in is sent to sign-in. People and access lists them with a note, and offers Member or Remove; there's no moving anyone to casual until it's back on.

### The demo

The demo is a site of its own with `DEMO=on`, its own database, and no Discord. The sign-in page has a button for each of the made-up members (Sam, Alex, Jordan and Casey, from `src/lib/demo.ts`) instead of Discord's, every page says it's a demo, and the nightly cron (`/api/cron/sync`) puts the group back as it was instead of syncing: it wipes the group's data and everyone's sessions and seeds the group again, dated from that day, so its nights are always coming up (`resetDemo` in `src/lib/demo-seed.ts`, the same data as `pnpm seed:dev`). One night is always on: it starts an hour before the reset with a warm-up game logged, and on the demo a night stays on for 26 hours (`nightGraceMs` in `src/lib/events.ts`), so it lasts until the next reset and visitors can log it as it happens. The nights before it are marked over. It needs no network, since the games' BGG details are in the seed. `DEMO` is ignored, and the reset refuses to run, with `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN` or `DISCORD_GUILD_ID` set, so a group's own site can't be wiped by mistake. The sign-in buttons only work for those four members. Everyone trying it shares one group, so it locks what one visitor could change for all the others: Group settings (`requireAdmin` refuses), prep links (`addPrepLink` and `removePrepLink` refuse) and ending the night that's on (`endNight` refuses).

To run one: a Vercel project and a Turso database of its own, with `DEMO=on`, `CASUAL_NIGHT=on` (so both kinds of night show; the nightly reset puts Group settings back to these variables, and makes Sam the admin), `DATABASE_URL`, `DATABASE_AUTH_TOKEN`, `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, `CRON_SECRET` and `APP_TIMEZONE`. The build applies the migrations; seed it the first time with `pnpm reset:demo` and those variables, or by calling the cron.

## How data gets in

| Source | How | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plays** | Log them on the Plays page. The app posts the same date / place / players / game message to the night's Discord channel, @-tagging people without pinging them. Its lines start with the bot's own symbols, which the parser reads by name, or 📅 / 📍 / 🤼 / 🎲 until they're uploaded. | Whenever |
| **Old or hand-written plays** | The bot reads the plays channels and parses the emoji template, or `Game: … / Players: …` lines. A night of several games can be written `🎲: [A](…) + [B](…)`, with commas, or as `🎲:` with one `- [Game](…)` per line below it. `+Chad` after the mentions is a guest. Claude handles anything else when it's on (`CLAUDE=on` and `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`). Games linked to BGG match exactly; plain titles are matched to the shelf, and leftovers land in a "needs a game match" queue. | Nightly cron, plus "Sync from Discord" |
| **Collections** | Each person saves their BGG username in Settings. Until the BGG API token is approved, use the CSV export instead. | Nightly cron, plus on save |
| **Opinions and rankings** | Rankings → Rate games, or any game page (love / like / meh / not for me, want to play, 🔥 must play), plus the ranking. | Whenever |
| **Nights** | Added on Plan or with `/event` in Discord, or made from logged plays. Each keeps an answer per person (`night_rsvp`, by player) and its post's ids (`game_night.messageId` / `threadId`). | As they change, on the site or in Discord |
| **Plans** | A night's page: propose, vote and lock in (core), or say what you're bringing (casual); prep links (the game's) and prep done. | Before a night |
| **Board Game Arena** | BGA's public game list page embeds every game as JSON with its BGG id (`src/lib/bga.ts`), so it's matched to the group's games exactly. No account or token needed. | Nightly cron, or "Load the game list" on Online |
| **Discovery catalog** | BGG's hot list plus a curated, rank-checked list of well-loved games, so Discover has unowned games to score even before anyone asks Claude. | Nightly cron, or "Load popular games" on Discover |
| **Coming up** | BGG's crowdfunding families and a geeklist of campaigns yet to open, through BGG's API. | Nightly cron |

**People.** Anyone in a play log is a *player*. A player is linked to a Discord account when they're @-mentioned in an imported message, when they sign in, when they use a night's buttons, or by hand in Settings. Someone added to a night on the site by name is a plain name on its post until then; linking them by hand tags them on the posts of the coming nights they've answered and adds them to those threads (`playerLinkedLater`). Settings also merges duplicates like "Sammy" and "Sam".

A player is their id; their name is only what's shown, so two people can both be called Lee. Wherever two players share a name, lists and pickers add when each started playing ("Lee (since Sep 2025)"); a last initial in Settings tells them apart for good. A name written in a hand-written post or a Discord name is matched by `matchByName` (`src/lib/players.ts`): a player's name, alias or member's account name. It never picks between two:
- a play log line that names a shared name seats the likeliest (a member before a guest, then whoever's been around longest) and asks *Who was it?* on Plays, a night at a time;
- signing in doesn't claim either, and starts a fresh player;
- naming them on a night's **Someone else…** asks to pick them from the list.

A name with only one player is still matched to them, though, so a new Lee in a post that just says "Lee" lands on the Lee who's played before. Settings' **Two people, one name?** splits it: tick the nights that were someone else and they become a new player's, with their answers for those nights.

## Where things live

- `src/config.ts`: the group's settings, as `loadSettings()` last read them, and the few that stay environment variables (server only)
- `src/lib/settings.ts` / `settings-store.ts`: what's on Group settings and how each value is checked (pure, tested), and reading and saving them
- `src/app/(app)/settings/group/`: the Group settings page and its admins
- `src/lib/play-parser.ts`: reads and writes the Discord play template (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/sync.ts`: Discord → plays, BGG → shelf, BGA → which games are online, matching games and players
- `src/lib/editions.ts`: editions of one game (BGG's reimplementations with the same name), shown once on the shelf (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/bga.ts`: reads Board Game Arena's game list page (pure parser, tested)
- `src/lib/events.ts` / `nights.ts`: loads nights with their answers, games and plays, and the time zone and attendance helpers (tested)
- `src/lib/calendar.ts` / `calendar-store.ts` / `calendar-message.ts`: nights in people's own calendars: the calendar files and add links and signed keys (pure, tested), the nights in someone's feed and its secret, and the private answer to Add to calendar on a post (pure, tested). `src/app/calendar/` has its pages and `src/app/api/calendar/` the files
- `src/lib/night-store.ts` / `rsvp.ts`: making, changing and answering the nights Boardroom runs, shared by the site's actions and the Discord handlers (the answer rules are pure, tested)
- `src/lib/night-message.ts` / `night-post.ts`: a night's post in Discord (pure, tested) and keeping it in step: `syncNightPost` posts, edits or moves it after every change
- `src/lib/announce-message.ts` / `announce-post.ts`: a night's announcement in the chat channel, with its dates on offer while its date's TBD (pure, tested), and keeping it in step; `night-post.ts` calls it
- `src/lib/icons.ts`: the symbols on the app's Discord posts, the Font Awesome icons in `assets/discord-emoji` that `pnpm discord:commands` uploads as the bot's own emoji, with plain emoji until then (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/night-title.ts`: naming a new night from the group's template, and swapping the locked-in game in and out of a name made that way (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/log-post.ts`: a night's log post in its plays channel and its copy in the night's thread: adding plays to it, and rewriting it after a play's edited, moved or deleted; `discord-message.ts` builds the card (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/nudges.ts` / `nudge-post.ts`: the reminder before a night and the morning-after DM asking for its plays: when each is due and what it says (pure, tested), and sending them from `/api/cron/tick`
- `src/lib/interactions.ts` / `discord-handlers.ts` / `discord-commands.ts` / `when.ts`: Discord's buttons, forms and `/event` (signature checks, ids and the command definitions are pure, tested), what each one does, and reading the dates and times people type, one or a list of dates to offer (tested). Discord POSTs them to `src/app/api/discord/interactions/route.ts`
- `src/lib/poll-store.ts` / `date-answers.ts` / `poll-message.ts` / `poll-post.ts`: finding a night's date: the reads and writes shared by the site and Discord, the answer rules (pure, tested), the post while the date's TBD (pure, tested; `syncNightPost` sends it), and what's said in Discord when the date's set or falls through
- `src/lib/night.ts` / `ranking.ts`: game-night scoring and the group ranking: how much of each person's list a game beats, averaged, and whose lists count for each kind of night (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/taste.ts` / `chance.ts`: taste profiles, and the calibrated chance someone likes a game (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/upcoming.ts`: Coming up: reading the geeklist's entries, what's still ahead, and which group a game goes in (pure, tested); `refreshUpcoming` in `catalog.ts` does the fetching
- `src/lib/pairwise.ts`: the ranking replay: picks the most informative "this or that?" and fits all the answers (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/ai.ts` / `insights.ts`: Claude calls and the group context they're given
- `src/lib/setup.ts`: the steps of getting set up, shared by Get set up and Home's banner (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/demo.ts` / `demo-seed.ts`: the made-up group of `pnpm seed:dev` and the demo, and when its nights fall (pure, tested), and writing it to the database
- `src/lib/welcome.ts` / `welcome-message.ts`: inviting someone to the core group's nights (the role, the hello and the DM), and what the bot says (pure, tested)
- `src/lib/bring-message.ts`: the DM asking someone to bring a game to a casual night, and its answers (pure, tested)
- `src/app/actions.ts`: every mutation (each one re-checks the session)
- `scripts/`: the sync, the dev seed and the demo's reset, registering Discord commands, the build's migration step, and bringing posts already in Discord up to date (`refresh-posts.ts`) or moving them to a new events channel (`move-posts.ts`)
